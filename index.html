Understanding Function Forms

Vertex Form

f(x) = a(x - h)² + k

Vertex Form makes it easy to identify the vertex and axis of symmetry.

Standard Form

f(x) = ax² + bx + c

Standard Form makes it easier to identify the y-intercept and work with the coefficients.

Comparing Vertex Form and Standard Form

Both forms represent the same quadratic function, but they show different information about the parabola.

Vertex Form

f(x) = a(x - h)² + k

Vertex Form makes it easy to identify the vertex and understand how the parabola moves.

Standard Form

f(x) = ax² + bx + c

Standard Form makes it easier to identify the y-intercept and the coefficients.

Example: Same Function, Different Forms

Vertex Form:

f(x) = 2(x - 3)² + 4

Standard Form:

f(x) = 2x² - 12x + 22

Both equations describe exactly the same parabola!

Step-by-Step: Converting Vertex Form to Standard Form

Start with Vertex Form:

f(x) = 2(x - 3)² + 4

Step 1: Expand the squared expression.

f(x) = 2(x² - 6x + 9) + 4

Step 2: Distribute the value of a.

f(x) = 2x² - 12x + 18 + 4

Step 3: Combine the constant terms.

f(x) = 2x² - 12x + 22

Final Answer: Standard Form!

Step-by-Step: Converting Standard Form to Vertex Form

Start with Standard Form:

f(x) = 2x² - 12x + 22

Step 1: Factor out the coefficient of x² from the first two terms.

f(x) = 2(x² - 6x) + 22

Step 2: Complete the square by adding and subtracting 9 inside the parentheses.

f(x) = 2(x² - 6x + 9 - 9) + 22

Step 3: Rewrite the perfect square trinomial.

f(x) = 2((x - 3)² - 9) + 22

Step 4: Distribute and simplify.

f(x) = 2(x - 3)² + 4

Final Answer: Vertex Form!

How Do a, h and k Affect the Graph?

The value of a

Controls the direction and width of the parabola.

The value of h

Moves the parabola left or right.

The value of k

Moves the parabola up or down.

Important Features of a Parabola

Vertex: (0, 0)

The vertex is the highest or lowest point of the parabola.

Axis of Symmetry: x = 0

The axis of symmetry is the vertical line that divides the parabola into two equal halves.

Opening: Upward

The opening tells us whether the parabola curves upward or downward.

Zeros (x-intercepts)

Zeros are the x-values where the graph crosses or touches the x-axis. At these points, f(x) = 0.
